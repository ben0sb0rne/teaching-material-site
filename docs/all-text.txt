EXPLORE DISSOLVING                 src/activities/explore-dissolving/index.html
================================================================================

Browser tab        Explore Dissolving
Page description   A particle model of what happens when a solid is stirred
                   into water.

The screen is one instrument on a bench. Both samples run at once, under the
same water, the same clock and the same collisions. There is no walkthrough
and nothing to click through: the student dispenses water and watches.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IDENTIFY READOUT                                       console, top left
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two lines: a name in capitals, and one sentence under it. Hovering an object
names it, focusing a control names it, and on a touchscreen a tap names it.
A tap on a key both names it and presses it.

Default, nothing selected
  Name             NO SELECTION
  Description      Select an object to identify it.

Every object that answers, with the description it gives:

  Water particle              Water, the solvent. Constantly moving and
                              colliding.
  Sugar particle (in pile)    Sugar, the solute. Held in the pile by attraction
                              to other sugar particles.
  Sugar particle (free)       Dissolved sugar. Part of the solution.
  Calcium carbonate particle  Calcium carbonate, the main ingredient in chalk.
                              Insoluble in water.
  Scale bar                   1 nanometre, one billionth of a metre.
  Viewport                    Magnified tens of millions of times.
  Register                    Particles dissolved. One light per particle.
  Counter                     Particles dissolved, out of 50.
  Clock                       Seconds since water was added.
  Freeze                      Pauses the simulation.
  Stir                        Spins the stir bars in both beakers.
  Reset                       Drains both beakers and adds fresh samples.
  Dispense water              Adds 250 mL of water to both beakers at once.
  Sugar                       Sugar, C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁.
  Calcium carbonate           Calcium carbonate, CaCO₃.
  Beaker                      250 mL beaker.
  Stir bar                    Magnetic stir bar. Spins when the stir plate is
                              on.
  Stir plate                  Magnetic stir plate. Spins the stir bar from
                              below.
  Probe                       Sends the magnified view to the viewport.
  Nozzle                      Water outlet.
  Powder chute                Adds a measured sample of powder.
  Water temperature           Hotter water is faster particles, so they hit
                              the solid harder and more often.
  Sample head                 Carries the water nozzle, the powder chute and
                              the probe.

  The names above are shown in capitals. "Probe" is written but not yet
  reachable: the head is one piece of art, so it answers for the whole
  assembly. Nozzle and powder chute answer from the streams that come out of
  them.

Water temperature slider                                 console, beside WATER
  Panel            WATER TEMP
  Value            20 °C         [+] the temperature now set, 20 to 100
  Ticks            20     60     100

  Continuous from 20 °C to 100 °C. The ticks mark the three the lesson
  compares; H jumps between them. 20 °C is the plate off, 100 °C is the boil.

Keyboard shortcuts. These work, but nothing on the cap says so: a real
instrument does not have letters printed under its buttons, and the caps read
as hardware only while they stay clean.
  Freeze           SPACE
  Stir             S
  Reset            R
  Dispense water   W
  Water temp       H          jumps to the next marked temperature, and wraps

  The slider also takes the arrow keys (1 degree), Page Up and Page Down
  (10 degrees) and Home and End once it has focus, and it can be dragged.

LAB NOTEBOOK                                           right of the bench
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Headings           Objectives
                   Challenges
                   Observations

The items below are PLACEHOLDERS. They exist so the notebook can be tested and
are meant to be replaced; the real objectives and challenges are still to be
written. Each one names an event, and writes its line into Observations when
that event happens.

Objectives
  1  Placeholder objective: add water to both beakers.
     writes → Added 250 mL of water to both samples.
  2  Placeholder objective: watch one sugar particle break free.
     writes → A sugar particle broke away from the pile.
  3  Placeholder objective: stir both beakers.
     writes → Stirred both beakers.

Challenges
  1  Placeholder prediction: will the calcium carbonate dissolve?
     choices  Yes   No
     writes → Recorded a prediction about calcium carbonate.
  2  Placeholder challenge: dissolve every sugar particle.
     writes → Every sugar particle dissolved.
  3  Placeholder challenge: identify a buried sugar particle.
     writes → Identified a particle buried inside the pile.

  A prediction has to be answered before it can be judged: the choice is
  recorded when it is tapped, and the item completes later, when what it
  predicted actually happens.

SPOKEN ALOUD                                      screen readers only, never seen
================================================================================

Each viewport carries a label
  Left             Magnified view of sugar in water
  Right            Magnified view of calcium carbonate in water

Progress, read at most every 4 seconds while the simulation runs:

  {8} of 50 sugar particles and {0} of 50 calcium carbonate particles have
  dissolved after {12} seconds.

Said once, when the instrument changes state:

  Water at 20 degrees. / Water at 60 degrees. / Water at 100 degrees.
  The water boiled away. The solid is left behind.
  Water added.
  Simulation frozen.
  Simulation resumed.
  Reset complete.
  Stirred the dry powder. Nothing dissolves until water is added.

  The last one is said when Stir is pressed before any water has been
  dispensed. The powder visibly shifts as well; with reduced motion it is
  left leaning to one side instead of rocking, so the stir still shows.

Said once, when a notebook item completes:

  Observed: {Every sugar particle dissolved.}

Said once, when a prediction is answered:

  Prediction recorded: {Yes}.
