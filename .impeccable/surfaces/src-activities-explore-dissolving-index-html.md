## Direction contract

THESIS: Dissolving is a count, not an impression. Every solute particle in the lens
has a matching flat glyph in the register beside it, so a student checks the quantity
by counting rather than being told it. It refuses the card-wrapped stage with a
summary number underneath, and it refuses minimalism: a surface that looks like real
science is what makes a thirteen-year-old read themselves as a scientist. It is a
running simulation, never a video: no transport, no timeline, and no end frame —
Reset returns the run to zero without stopping it, because a surface with no Play
button has nothing to offer a student who resets into a frozen field. The second
refusal is the slide deck: nothing in the walkthrough seeks, pauses or cuts. The
instrument is not a frame around the content; it is the interface.

OWN-WORLD: A microscope, built out of a dark instrument plate carrying flat saturated
colour, after mid-century science publishing (How & Why, Modern Plastics, n+m 1969).
The panel reads top to bottom as eyepiece, field, stage: a head naming the figure and
the specimen, a recessed well holding the field, and a tray of glass slides below it,
the seated one lifted and lit from under. The field is an aperture, not a circle on a
page — vignetted, fringed warm and cool at the field stop, and carrying a reticle
etched on the eyepiece rather than floating in the specimen, which is why the
crosshair and its 1 nm scale bar are drawn after the vignette and never dimmed by it.
The scale bar replaced a ×1,000,000 chip that claimed a number which was never right. Ground #070B10 under three
blurred orbital petals that bloom and add where they cross, switchable off entirely.
Hairline keylines, no drop shadows, no halftone, no perspective. Jost self-hosted.
Colour roles that never swap: --solvent is water only, --solute the dissolving solid
only, --signal attention, --data secondary measurement; --block and --signal-fg exist
because a fill and a foreground cannot be one token when the ground flips. The only
modelled object in the world is the sphere: sharp, uniform, hard specular, bounce
light on the lower limb. The second is the stirrer, and it is the one depth cue the
plate allows: an object so close to the objective that it is nothing but a soft mass,
drawn inside the lens canvas so the circle clips it by construction. The engine can
cage solvent around a freed solute, but this surface runs with that off: the cages
turned every freed particle into a slow clump and the solution stopped mixing.
Everything else, the register glyphs included, is flat, because Isotype counts flat
identical units and a glossy glyph reads as a picture of a particle rather than as
one.

STORY: The student arrives to a simulation already running under a FIG. plate head.
Red particles break off the pile and disperse through the water, and the register
fills in step. The log beside it names one thing to catch at a time and records when
it happened; twice, deliberately, the thing never happens, and that line sitting on
"still watching" for as long as you care to look is what insoluble means. They leave
knowing dissolving is particles separating and dispersing through the solvent, that
it is countable, and that the water never stopped hitting either solid.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Title with the second word in signal and a unit chip; nothing else in
the top strip, because the substance switch moved into the instrument. Lens plate left
inside a panel whose head is the eyepiece label and whose foot is the slide tray.
Right column, top to bottom: particle register, controls plate carrying elapsed time
with Stir and Reset, then the observation log. No toolbar and no timeline anywhere. A spoon rests in the beaker at all
times, because a spoon standing in a glass of water is an ordinary sight; the one in
the lens appears only on the stroke, because a spoon permanently parked in a view
magnified a million times is not.

Specimens are a tablist, not a radio group: a radio group answers "which one" and a
tablist answers "which view", and this switches what the instrument is looking at.
Arrow keys and a roving tabindex, so the whole tray is one Tab stop.

Signature interaction: the register fills in lockstep with particles leaving the pile,
so the chart and the simulation are read as one act. Second to it, Stir moves both
spoons off one phase held in the model, so they are on the same stroke by construction
rather than by two animations agreeing. The stroke is a circular translation, not a
rotation: the bowl travels the vessel while the spoon holds its orientation, the way a
hand does. It opens from rest, runs two and a quarter decelerating turns, spirals back
in, and ends exactly where it began, so the resting spoon is never left parked
off-centre. The beaker is seen from the side, so its circle foreshortens to an
ellipse.
Both spoons are placeholders for raster art, and the swap is a change of shape only —
the phase binding stays.

FORM: The Vienna Method (Isotype), chosen as IMPECCABLE'S PICK over the roll's
assignment, rendered against mid-century science-publishing references the user
supplied. Isotype supplies the method (flat countable units); the references supply
the surface. seed:0bb7edb8bba47106
